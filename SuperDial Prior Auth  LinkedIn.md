A prior authorization can be clinically appropriate and still get delayed because one required piece of information is missing.  
  
Earlier this month at [****Informa****](https://www.linkedin.com/company/informa/) 's Hub West 2026, our VP of Product, [****Entela Duka****](https://www.linkedin.com/in/entela-duka/), moderated a panel with [****Ellis Wheby****](https://www.linkedin.com/in/elliswheby/) of Tarsus Pharmaceuticals and [****Melissa Polvi RN BA PACS****](https://www.linkedin.com/in/melissa-polvi-rn-ba-pacs-24404361/) of Insmed on reducing delays in the PA process.  
  
A few practical lessons from the discussion:  
  
1\. Get the first submission right. Melissa emphasized using a checklist to make sure required labs, imaging, treatment history, and other documentation are included.  
  
2\. Know the plan-specific requirements. Ellis highlighted that forms, tests, and supporting documentation can vary by plan, so teams need to identify what applies before submitting.  
  
3\. Use automation to catch gaps early. The panel discussed building checks into workflows to flag missing documentation and other requirements before submission, while keeping human judgment for more complex cases.  
  
At SuperDial, our AI agents can automate parts of the prior authorization workflow, including retrieving requirements, checking status, identifying missing documentation, and determining next steps.

AI Is Expanding Across Healthcare Operations  
  
Healthcare AI is moving beyond individual chatbots and isolated use cases toward connected tools, automated workflows, and AI agents that can support organizations across clinical and administrative operations.  
  
Recent developments in enterprise healthcare AI highlight several important areas:  
  
🔹 AI Agents for Continuous Work  
New AI agents can work continuously on defined tasks, helping teams automate repetitive activities and keep important workflows moving with less manual intervention.  
🔹 Cloud Based AI Development  
Advanced AI coding tools are allowing technology teams to develop and manage applications remotely, creating new opportunities to build healthcare specific solutions more efficiently.  
🔹 Connected Applications & Data  
Healthcare organizations can increasingly connect approved applications to shared digital environments, allowing teams to work with relevant data and tools while maintaining organizational permissions.  
🔹 Workflow Automation  
AI can support administrative processes such as documentation, information gathering, prior authorization, scheduling, and other repetitive tasks.  
🔹 Healthcare Enterprise Adoption  
AI platforms are increasingly being designed specifically for hospitals and health systems, bringing AI closer to everyday clinical and operational workflows.  
  
The potential impact extends across:  
  
📌 Clinical documentation  
📌 Patient access  
📌 Prior authorization  
📌 Medical coding  
📌 Claims and billing  
📌 Revenue cycle management  
📌 Healthcare IT  
📌 Data analysis  
📌 Administrative operations  
  
For RCM teams, the opportunity is particularly significant.  
  
AI can help reduce repetitive manual work, organize information, identify workflow bottlenecks, and allow experienced professionals to focus on complex cases, exceptions, and decisions that require human judgment.  
  
At the same time, healthcare organizations must maintain strong:  
  
🔐 Data security  
🛡️ Privacy controls  
👥 Access permissions  
📋 Governance  
👨⚕️ Human oversight
