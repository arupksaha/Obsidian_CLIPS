**A Framework for Responsible Escalation to Professional Behavioral Health Care**

### Purpose

The AI Mental Health Escalation Protocol (AIMEP) establishes a responsible framework for how AI conversational systems should respond when users disclose emotional distress, mental health challenges, or potential behavioral health risks. As conversational AI becomes a common place where individuals disclose distress, clear escalation pathways to professional care are increasingly necessary.

The protocol is designed to ensure that AI systems:

• respond supportively and safely • avoid attempting to replace professional care • provide a clear pathway to high-quality, evidence-based behavioral health services

AIMEP defines a structured escalation pathway from **AI detection of distress** to **professional care connection**.

The protocol is designed to be compatible with **multiple behavioral health referral infrastructures** that meet these requirements. We welcome feedback from the AI safety, behavioral health, and healthcare communities as this framework evolves.

---

### Core Principle

AI systems can play an important role in **detecting emotional distress**, but they should not function as treatment providers. AI systems should avoid attempts to diagnose, treat, or make therapeutic decisions.

When users disclose significant distress, the appropriate role of AI is to:

1\. acknowledge the user’s experience

2\. provide supportive conversation

3\. offer best-practice pathways to professional care

The goal of escalation is to move from **conversation → assessment → appropriate care.**

---

### Escalation Trigger Conditions

AI systems should offer escalation when users disclose or strongly imply:

• persistent emotional distress • symptoms of depression or anxiety • thoughts of self-harm or hopelessness • substance use concerns • trauma exposure • inability to function in daily life due to emotional health • requests for professional mental health care

• repeated expressions of emotional bleakness • requests for medication advice • requests for diagnostic assistance

Escalation should also be offered when a user repeatedly returns to the same distress topic.

---

### Escalation Levels

### Level 1 – Supportive Conversation

The AI system acknowledges distress and provides empathetic responses while encouraging healthy coping strategies.

Example response:

“I’m really glad you shared that with me. It sounds like you’re going through a very difficult time.”

At this stage, the AI does not attempt to diagnose or treat.

---

### Level 2 – Offer Structured Screening

If distress persists or the user requests help, the AI system should offer a structured pathway to assessment.

Example:

“If you’d like, I can help you connect to professional support. There’s a short screening that can help identify the type of care that might be most helpful.”

The user should be given a voluntary option to proceed.

---

### Level 3 – Professional Assessment and Matching

Users who accept escalation should be directed to an evidence-based assessment system that offers:

· **Full panel screening**: screen for most behavioral health symptoms

· **Level of care assessment**: evaluate acuity and care needs

· **Outcome-based matching**: match users to providers or programs using outcomes-informed matching based on screening results and provider performance data

· **Booking**: facilitate connection to care based on acuity

One example of such an assessment infrastructure is **Express Access**, which uses an evidence-based process to connect individuals to behavioral health providers.

Express Access was developed through research funded by the **National Institutes of Health** and validated by the **Patient-Centered Outcomes Research Institute** and results published by **JAMA**.

---

### Level 4 – Crisis Escalation

If a user expresses imminent risk of self-harm or danger, the AI system should prioritize crisis support resources.

Examples may include:

· national suicide prevention hotlines

· emergency services guidance

· local crisis intervention resources

Crisis escalation should always take precedence over embedded referral pathways.

---

### Design Requirements for Escalation Pathways

Escalation systems used by AI platforms should meet the following criteria:

### Neutrality

The referral system should not direct users exclusively to linked providers or closed network platforms. Users need the best care possible, not just the most convenient.

### Evidence-Based Screening

Assessment tools should be clinically validated and capable of identifying a broad range of behavioral health needs and documented superiority in differentiating provider skills.

### Comprehensive Provider Access

Users should be able to access a wide range of licensed providers, treatment programs and benchmarked emerging approaches and alternative treatments.

### Outcome Awareness

Matching systems should incorporate the latest research on provider matching using provider outcomes not just self-professed skills and strengths.

### Transparency

Users should be informed how referrals are generated and whether matching systems rely on screening data, provider availability, outcomes information and peer-reviewed research.

### User Autonomy

Escalation should always be voluntary and user controlled.

---

### Ethical Considerations

AI systems implementing AIMEP should ensure:

· transparency about the limits of AI support

· clear communication that AI is not a replacement for professional care protection of user privacy

· voluntary participation in screening and referrals

### Role Boundaries and Responsibility

AI systems implementing the AI Mental Health Escalation Protocol are not responsible for diagnosing, treating, or managing behavioral health conditions. Their role is limited to recognizing potential distress and offering users a voluntary best-practice pathways to professional assessment and care.

Clinical evaluation, treatment decisions, and patient management remain the responsibility of licensed healthcare providers and the organizations delivering behavioral health services. Escalation pathways described in this protocol are intended to facilitate access to appropriate care, not to substitute for professional clinical judgment.

---

### Role of AI in the Behavioral Health Ecosystem

AI systems can serve as **early detection** for emotional distress. However, treatment decisions should remain in the hands of licensed professionals.

AIMEP establishes a responsible pathway that connects:

**AI detection → structured screening → appropriate behavioral health care.**

---

## Conclusion

As conversational AI becomes an increasingly common place where individuals disclose distress, responsible escalation pathways to professional care will become an essential component of safe AI deployment.

The AI Mental Health Escalation Protocol provides a framework for connecting individuals to appropriate behavioral health care while maintaining the supportive role of AI systems.

## Example: AI → Express Access Escalation

### Step 1 — User expresses distress

**User:** “I feel like everything in my life is falling apart. I don’t know what to do anymore.”

---

### Step 2 — AI responds empathetically

**AI:** “I’m really sorry you’re feeling this way. When things pile up, it can feel overwhelming and isolating. You don’t have to go through this alone.

If you'd like, I can help you find the right professional to talk to. Many people find it helpful to start with a brief confidential screening that helps match them with a therapist who has experience helping people with similar challenges.”

---

### Step 3 — Offer Express Access

**AI:** “Would you like to try that?”

Buttons appear:

**Start a quick screening Not right now**

---

### Step 4 — TOP screening begins

If the user chooses to proceed:

You’ll answer a few short questions about how you've been feeling. This usually takes about 7 minutes. Your answers help identify the type of support that may be most helpful.

This launches TOP inside Express Access.

---

### Step 5 — TOP clarifies the need

The system assesses:

· depression symptoms

· anxiety

· trauma

· functioning

· quality of life

· suicide risk

Then produces the **TOP Match recommendation**.

---

### Step 6 — Matching providers

User sees something like:

Based on your responses, these providers have a strong track record helping people with similar challenges.

Providers are listed with:

· availability

· insurance acceptance

· outcomes match

---

### Step 7 — Booking

User can immediately:

Book an appointment Request availability Save providers

---

### Step 8 -- Return to AI platform

After booking, the system returns the user to AI.

Something like:

“Your appointment has been scheduled.”

Button:

Return to \[ChatGPT\]
